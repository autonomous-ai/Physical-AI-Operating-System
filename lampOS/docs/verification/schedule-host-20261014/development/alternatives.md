## Reminders: repeat, postpone, complete, and missed items

**Apple Reminders/Siri.** Apple documents Siri requests containing a task, a date/time, or a daily repetition. Date-only reminders notify at 09:00 by default, with a configurable all-day notification time. Completing an item and deleting it are separate actions. [iPhone/iPad guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102484) The Mac notification UI separately supports ignoring a notification, completing the task, and choosing when to be reminded again. The page does not enumerate universal snooze durations or establish a Siri voice-snooze contract. [Mac notifications](https://support.apple.com/en-mt/guide/reminders/remn4e53b572/mac) Mac settings can show all-day items as overdue starting the next day and include overdue items in the badge. That is overdue visibility, not a promise of replaying every missed sound. [Mac settings](https://support.apple.com/en-mide/guide/reminders/remn4a9a4fe6/mac)

**Google Tasks through voice assistants.** Gemini for Home’s examples include a reminder tomorrow at noon and a weekly Wednesday evening reminder. Voice commands can change an item’s time, complete it, or delete it. Shared-device setup uses Voice Match and personal results; the page directs users to Tasks or Calendar on the phone for alerts. [Voice Tasks guide](https://support.google.com/googlehome/answer/16722329) Tasks/Calendar support recurring schedules and an end date. After completing an occurrence, the next appears on its specified date; Calendar distinguishes deleting one occurrence, this and following occurrences, or the series. [Repeating tasks](https://support.google.com/tasks/answer/12132599?hl=en) Calendar exposes uncompleted tasks from the preceding 365 days and documents 09:00 notifications for tasks without a time. [Calendar tasks](https://support.google.com/tasks/answer/9901136?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en) Google also warns that Android may delay or suppress notifications for infrequently used apps. [Notification settings](https://support.google.com/tasks/answer/13486720?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en) The reviewed voice guide documents rescheduling, not a task-snooze command or an offline catch-up policy.

**Alexa consumer reminders.** Amazon explicitly documents “Snooze reminder for 20 minutes” and “Remind me again in an hour,” daily/weekly reminders, and querying reminders. Optional follow-up reminders notify hourly or daily until the user confirms completion. Missed reminders are represented by an Echo yellow light or screen notification, with a voice query to retrieve them. The original Alexa guide says reminders on a reset/deregistered device are lost. [Alexa reminders](https://digprjsurvey.amazon.com/csad/help/node/GVMW5E8YCNR6AC6E) The separate Alexa+ guide says reminders announce and notify the phone, default to the device that created them, and **may** be lost on reset/deregistration. It also says household members can see tasks/reminders. [Alexa+ guide](https://digprjsurvey.amazon.com/csad/help/node/T30e4eImkYHXKiYbvr) Neither page defines a power-outage replay deadline, deduplication guarantee, or how snoozing interacts with the next recurring occurrence.

**Alexa skill wording is an explicit contract.** Skills must obtain permission for each reminder and clarify its time and recurrence. When due, Alexa plays a tone and speaks the reminder twice unless stopped; the skill cannot customize the voice delivery. A reminder may interrupt an ongoing interaction. The documentation distinguishes relative offsets from absolute scheduling, and says completed reminders remain visible in the app for three days. [Skills reminder overview](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/docs/alexa/smapi/alexa-reminders-overview.html) These are skill rules, not evidence that another product should copy Alexa’s interruption policy.

## Explicit pitfalls and unresolved questions

- **Civil time is not elapsed duration.** Jiff exposes gaps and folds when civil time maps to zero or two instants. Its default `Compatible` chooses the earlier instant in a fold and shifts forward through a gap; `Reject`, `Earlier`, and `Later` are available. This is a library default, not evidence of any assistant’s policy. [Disambiguation API](https://docs.rs/jiff/latest/jiff/tz/enum.Disambiguation.html)
- **A zone is more than a fixed UTC offset.** Rules change. Jiff’s zoned parser rejects conflicts between an explicit offset and the named zone by default; other resolution policies exist. [Offset conflicts](https://docs.rs/jiff/latest/jiff/tz/enum.OffsetConflict.html) The target lamp’s installed tzdata version, update mechanism, and configured zone remain unverified.
- **Wall clocks can move backwards.** Rust explicitly says `SystemTime` is non-monotonic. `Instant` is opaque and does not specify whether suspension counts as elapsed time across platforms/Rust versions. [SystemTime](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/time/struct.SystemTime.html), [Instant](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/time/struct.Instant.html) Reboot recovery and clock correction therefore remain separate unanswered product questions; the reviewed assistant guides do not disclose their implementation.
- **“Committed” depends on persistence settings and storage.** SQLite documents that WAL with `synchronous=NORMAL` can lose a committed transaction after system crash/power loss; WAL with `FULL` adds a sync per commit. [Synchronous modes](https://www.sqlite.org/pragma.html#pragma_synchronous) WAL requires same-machine shared memory and allows one writer at a time. [WAL](https://www.sqlite.org/wal.html) SQLite’s atomic-commit guarantees assume the OS/storage honors flush operations. [Atomic commit](https://www.sqlite.org/atomiccommit.html) Inference: durable scheduling records alone do not demonstrate exactly-once audible delivery or that a person heard an alert.
- **Unknown across these consumer docs:** catch-up after long downtime, suppression windows for very late timers, DST fold/gap choice, concurrent snooze versus repeat, cancellation racing already-playing audio, and delivery receipt semantics. No internal architecture, lamp latency, or acoustic result is inferred. This preparation ran no tests or benchmarks.
