# The name, heard as a bare "Lamp"

A name model for `lamp-name` that hears "Hey Lamp", "Lamp ơi" and a bare
"Lamp" at the start of a sentence ("Lamp, what time is it?"), and is made
not to be set off by "Lan", "Liam", "lamb", "land", "plan", "lab", "Sam" or
"ma'am" in the same place, by ordinary talk, or by the lamp's own voice. It
takes the input of the model `lamp-name` runs now and gives the same kind
of score, so it can replace that file with no code change.

This is task 5 of [the builders' list](work-split.md), issue
[#694](https://github.com/autonomous-ai/lampOS/issues/694). Its first model
(#725, SHA-256 `f376e964…`) was tried on lamp-4ace on 2026-10-14 and not
installed: it heard calls far less often than the lamp's own model. This
document is about the second model, made after that check, and what limits
it. Everything here was trained and measured on a laptop (Apple M2 Max,
macOS 26.6), and the model's graph was run under tract 0.23.8 there and in
a Linux ARM64 container. Nothing here was run on a lamp, and no number
here is the lamp's.

## Answer

- **On the lamp, the first model failed for a reason found here.** It was
  taught to tell "Lamp" from "Lamb", "Lan" and the rest with near misses
  that made up 54 % of its negatives of other words, and learned a "Lamp"
  too narrow for voices unlike the ones it was trained on: in the Mac's
  own `say` voices, which no model here learned from, it heard
  2.3 of the played check's six calls, and with a loudspeaker and a room
  2.0 (on the lamp: 1). Drawn as a tenth of those negatives instead, the
  same data and trainer hear about twice as many calls in those voices.
- **The second model**, `models/name/name.onnx` (1,233,449 bytes, SHA-256
  `a8453aebf6d61c6e1f5d05c1519b45a272bc9198d0b9c5f315fdca579ba900dd`), is
  the mean of three such models (three seeds). On the played check's
  lines in eleven `say` voices at three speeds (33 speakers), at 0.9 as
  the lamp uses it, it would pass the first builder's bar (at least three
  of the six calls, at most one of the eight near misses) for 22 of 33
  speakers as said and 12 of 33 played through a loudspeaker into a room;
  the first model for 9 and 8.
- **The limit is the same one syllable.** In voices it never learned
  from, every setting tried trades a bare "Lamp" for "Lan", "Lâm",
  "lamb" and "lab". Without near misses in training the six calls are
  heard (6.0 of 6) and so are five of the eight near misses; with every
  near miss, as the first model was trained, 2.5 calls and 0.5 near
  misses. Played
  through a loudspeaker into a room, no recipe tried passes the bar for
  more than 13 of 33 speakers at 0.9 (18 of 33 for the best single seed
  at the threshold best for it, 0.92). At 0.9 the new model takes "Lan,
  ..." in about a third of the voices, "Lâm" and "lamb" generated by
  Piper in 40 to 51 %.
- **Against talk it does better than the first model** at the same 0.9:
  1.4 hearings an hour of LibriSpeech readers (3.3 before), 15.6 an hour
  of Vietnamese (23.4), 21.6 of Vietnamese speakers of English (36.2), 5.9
  of meetings (10.1).
- **It could not be measured beside the lamp's own model**, whose file
  is not in the repository or on this machine. The first builder's played
  check is the comparison that counts: run it on this file (below).
  `training/name/combine.py beside` can also put this model beside the
  lamp's, in one file, if that file is shared.

## Why the first model heard so few: the played check imitated

The recording is deleted, so [`played.py`](../training/name/played.py)
says the same lines again with the Mac's own voices, on this Mac. Of the
29 English voices `say` lists, eleven sound different here (the "Eddy"
family fall back to Samantha's or Daniel's sound; Aman and Tara give
silence until downloaded): Albert, Daniel, Fred, Junior, Karen, Kathy,
Moira, Ralph, Rishi, Samantha and Tessa, each at its own speed and at 150
and 230 words a minute, 33 speakers. The two Vietnamese lines are said by
Linh. "Lan, ..." and "Sam, ..." were written so in #694; here a question
follows them. Each line is heard "as said" and "played": through
microphone.py's loudspeaker at the middle of its ranges, a room, and the
first 100 ms after each silence 30 dB down. A speaker passes when at
least three of the six calls are heard and at most one of the eight near
misses. Apple's voices are used for testing only; nothing here is
trained on them.

The first model heard 2 of Samantha's six calls at her own speed as said,
and 0 played; on the lamp it heard 1. Over all 33 speakers it heard 2.3
as said and 2.0 played. The imitation tells the models apart as the lamp
did, though its numbers are not the lamp's.

**The near misses are what did it.** The first model's negatives of other
words were 146,268 inputs of near misses ("Lan", "lamb"..., "lamp" inside
a sentence, the lamp-like replies) and 126,513 of other words. The same
inputs, trained in the flat shape for 6,000 steps with seed 0, with the
near misses drawn as a set share of those negatives (`--near-share`):

| Near misses among the other words | As said: speakers passing | calls of 6 | near misses of 8 | Played: speakers passing | calls of 6 | near misses of 8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| none | 0/33 | 6.0 | 5.2 | 0/33 | 5.9 | 5.5 |
| a tenth | 22/33 | 4.4 | 0.9 | 10/33 | 4.1 | 1.4 |
| 15 % | 18/33 | 3.5 | 0.6 | 7/33 | 3.3 | 1.1 |
| a fifth | 19/33 | 3.8 | 0.9 | 11/33 | 3.1 | 1.1 |
| 30 % | 14/33 | 2.8 | 0.5 | 3/33 | 2.2 | 1.0 |
| all, as they come (54 %, as the first model) | 12/33 | 2.5 | 0.5 | 5/33 | 1.9 | 0.7 |

All at a score of 0.9 for one step. More near misses, fewer calls heard in
voices the model never learned from; fewer near misses, and they are all
heard as calls. A tenth passes the most speakers.

## What exists and what we take

**The lamp's own name model** runs openWakeWord's mel and embedding models
and then `name.onnx`, "a small classifier trained on generated speech of
people saying 'Hey Lamp' and 'Lamp ơi', and of people saying other things"
([name.md](name.md)). A hearing is a score of 0.9 or more (`--least`) for
one step, said when the score falls or after 0.8 s, then 1 s passed over
(`crates/name/src/said.rs`). Its file lives on the lamp in
`/opt/lampOS/name/models/`; its training is outside the repository.

- Taken: the same input, output and rule, so that the new file replaces
  `name.onnx` with no code change; the training and measuring of task 3
  ([stop-word.md](stop-word.md)) and of the first model.
- Changed: near misses are a tenth of each batch's other words, not as
  many as there are; the flat shape; the mean of three seeds.

**Short names are known to be hard.** Picovoice advises against wake words
of fewer than six phonemes and suggests putting "Hey" or "OK" before a
short word ([Picovoice](https://picovoice.ai/docs/tips/choosing-a-wake-word/);
seen in a search summary). "Lamp" is four phonemes, "Hey Lamp" seven.
Apple let people say just "Siri" from iOS 17, at first in English in four
countries ([iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2023/06/06/ios-17-siri-invocation/)),
with later checks behind a low-power first detector
([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/voice-trigger)).

- Taken: a second check is what makes a short name workable elsewhere;
  here the lamp's policy weighs the name with where the person looks and
  what was said ([policy.md](policy.md)).

**Voices from another generator.** openWakeWord trains on one generator
(Piper) with its speakers blended. Two more were looked at:

- [Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M) v1.0 (Apache 2.0
  weights; trained on public-domain and permissively licensed audio and
  on synthetic audio from closed providers' voices, by its model card),
  run as ONNX from
  [onnx-community](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX),
  54 voices in eight languages. Taken for testing: a generator the new
  model never heard. Tried in training and not kept ("What was tried").
  One of its voices, `af_kore`, has the name of Gemini's "Kore", the
  lamp's own voice; whether it sounds like it is not stated anywhere.
- Other Piper voices: by their model cards, most English ones were
  fine-tuned from the Lessac voice (research-only data) or the Ryan voice
  (non-commercial); VCTK, ARCTIC and the British-accent sets among them.
  Besides LibriTTS (trained on) and Cori and LJ Speech (kept for testing),
  three are trained on public-domain LibriVox recordings (Kristin, John,
  Norman); they were not tried, being the same kind of generator.
- The Mac's `say` voices: for testing only (Apple's licence is not for
  training a model we ship), as the first builder's check used them.

**Speech of Vietnamese speakers of English, and of "Lamp ơi".** As in the
first round, none was found that a shipped model may be trained on
(L2-ARCTIC and the Speech Accent Archive are non-commercial, Common Voice
needs an account, EdAcc is share-alike and kept for testing). The stand-in
is the English LibriTTS voices given the phonemes espeak's Vietnamese
voice reads from the English words, for a third of all lines; Linh, the
Mac's Vietnamese voice, says "Lamp ơi" and the Vietnamese near misses in
testing.

**The lamp's microphone chain.** lamp-4ace runs the echo canceller
(sonora's AEC3, which keeps its high-pass filter) with noise suppression
off (`deploy/lamp-4ace/lamp-voice.service`), so with nothing playing,
`mic2/audio` is close to what the microphone hears. Nothing of it was
added beyond the first round's lost onset and loudspeaker.

## What was made

`lampOS/training/name/` (the first round's code, with these changes) and
`lampOS/training/stop/train.py` (three options, all off by default):

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `training/stop/train.py` | `--near-sets` and `--near-share`: the near misses drawn as a set share of the other-word quarter of each batch. `--leave-out` (whole sets) and `--leave-out-way` (now one or more ways). Inputs made with different lists of sets keep their set names apart. |
| `training/name/played.py` | The played check imitated in the Mac's voices: `make`, `score MODEL...`, `verdict FILE`. |
| `training/name/combine.py` | Models put into one file `lamp-name` runs as it runs one: `mean` (on the logit scale, the threshold movable) and `beside` (the larger of two scores). |
| `training/name/kokoro.py` | Kokoro run as ONNX, with Piper's espeak phonemes; `speak.py --voice kokoro`. |
| `training/name/make_data.py` | Unchanged by default: task 5's inputs. `NAME_KOKORO=on` and `NAME_VOICE_CHANGE=on` make the inputs that were tried. |
| `training/name/evaluate.py`, `tables.py` | Kokoro's test voices as a held-out generator. |
| `training/name/make.sh`, `make_speech.sh`, `clips.py`, `kokoro.sha256` | The new model from nothing; Kokoro's files pinned to one revision and checked. |

**The model.** Task 5's inputs exactly (generated calls, near misses and
replies by 678 Piper LibriTTS voices, MSWC's "lamp" and near-miss words by
real people, other words, 13 h of talk with FLEURS Vietnamese and accented
generated talk, the lost onset, loudspeaker and room; [the first round's
"What was made"](#the-first-round-in-brief)). Trained three times, seeds 0,
1 and 2, in the flat shape (Flatten, Gemm 1,536 to 64, Relu, Gemm 64 to 64,
Relu, Gemm 64 to 1, Sigmoid), 6,000 steps, negatives weighed 3 to 1, near
misses a tenth of each batch's other words. The file is their mean on the
logit scale, then a sigmoid (`combine.py mean`): 307,779 weights, ONNX
opset 13, IR 8, made of Flatten, Gemm, Relu, Sigmoid, Clip, Sub, Div, Log,
Sum, Mul, Add and Identity. The mean is there so as not to depend on one
seed, and is not the best of them on every count: the three alone pass
the played check for 22, 21 and 19 of 33 speakers as said, 10, 6 and 9
played, the mean 22 and 12.

## The first round in brief

The first model's data are the second's: 12,000 calls by 791 Piper
LibriTTS voices (1,685 of them by 113 voices kept for validation), three
ways of calling, a third read with espeak's Vietnamese phonemes; 9,000
generated near misses of twelve kinds; 202 MSWC "lamp" words by real
people (each eight times) and 6,714 near-miss words; 17 LibriSpeech
utterances with "lamp" inside (each twenty times); 1,500 lamp-like
replies by the female voices; Speech Commands and MSWC other words; 13 h
of talk (LibriSpeech readers, generated talk and replies, accented
generated talk, FLEURS Vietnamese). Each example is 2.59 s, nine inputs
80 ms apart; an input is a call when the name ended 0.02 to 0.74 s before
the end of what it sees; every short clip is placed with its word ending
at the same moment. Done to the voice at random: the first 60 to 140 ms
after a silence 20 to 40 dB down (half), a small loudspeaker (two in
five), a room (three in ten); around it the microphone's floor, other
talk or noise. FLEURS was first read as silence (fixed then, before the
first model). The first model is in `lamp-voice` at commit `313aee7e7`
(`git show 313aee7e7:lampOS/models/name/name.onnx`).

## Wiring it on the lamp

The kind: a model file, off unless set. Nothing installs it; every lamp
hears as before until a file is put in `lamp-name`'s models directory.

**First, the played check (about ten minutes):** the first builder's
namecheck steps of `busrecord.sh`, with a directory holding the two
front-end files and this `name.onnx`:

    lamp-name check THAT_DIR RECORDING.wav

over a new recording of the same played set, beside the lamp's model. The
bar is #694's: at least 3 of 6 calls offline (4 of 6 live), and no more
false hits than the lamp's model. `training/stop/listen.py` does the same
on a Mac at any threshold.

If it passes:

1. Keep the current file: `mv /opt/lampOS/name/models/name.onnx /opt/lampOS/name/models/name-hey.onnx`.
2. Copy `models/name/name.onnx` to `/opt/lampOS/name/models/name.onnx`.
3. Leave the threshold at its default, 0.9, one step. For fewer false
   hearings, at the cost of calls, `--least 0.95` (tables above).
4. Restart `lamp-name.service`. To go back, put `name-hey.onnx` back.

Whatever takes `name/heard` should pass over hearings while the lamp's own
reply plays: a reply that starts "Lamp here, ..." sets it off.

## Where it is most likely to be wrong on the lamp

1. **"Lan", "Lâm" and "lamb".** It takes "Lan, ..." in about a third of
   voices it never heard, and generated "Lâm" in half. A colleague called
   Lan or Lâm will set it off in this office; the policy has to weigh it.
2. **A voice through a loudspeaker.** Played, it hears about 3.5 of 6
   calls and passes the bar for 12 of 33 speakers; the lamp's own check
   may land anywhere in that spread, and which `say` voice it used is not
   known here.
3. **A person.** Every call measured is generated, or a real "lamp" cut
   out of a read sentence (55 % heard). No one said "Lamp," to it.
4. **The lamp's own voice.** Kokoro's `af_kore`, Piper's women, Cori and
   LJ Speech stand in for Gemini's "Kore"; Gemini's voice through the
   lamp's loudspeaker and canceller was not heard.

## How to make it again

From `lampOS/training/name/`, with `uv`, a network and about 60 GB of disk
for a while:

    sh make.sh

It runs training/stop's `make.sh` as far as the sets both tasks use, then
downloads its own (checked against
[`downloads.sha256`](../training/name/downloads.sha256) and
[`kokoro.sha256`](../training/name/kokoro.sha256)), generates the speech
(`make_speech.sh`: Piper's voices, and Kokoro's test voices), gathers the
sets (`clips.py`), makes task 5's inputs (`make_inputs.sh`), trains the
three models and their mean, measures it and prints the tables; on a Mac
it also says the played check's lines and scores them (`played.py`). The
three trainings take about three minutes each on three threads when the
machine is free (an hour when it is not); the measurements about an hour.
The tried inputs: `NAME_KOKORO=on sh make_speech.sh` for Kokoro's training
sets, then `NAME_KOKORO=on [NAME_VOICE_CHANGE=on] NAME_INPUTS=FOLDER sh
make_inputs.sh words talk-kokoro check`.
